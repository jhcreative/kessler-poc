## 4. Architecture: one source of truth, a one-way update flow

```
 views (React Router 7) ──command──▶ FastAPI module router ──▶ Postgres: jobs + job_events (one transaction)
        ▲                                                         │
        └──────────── websocket: event envelope ◀── in-process hub ◀┘   (after commit)
```

- **Commands in, events out.** A view never writes to another view. It sends a command, such as
  `approve_for_dispatch`, `assign_tech`, `set_needs_review`, `close_job` or `approve_invoice`.
  - The backend validates the command against the state machine and the contract rails.
  - It writes the job and an append-only `job_events` row in one transaction.
  - After the commit, it publishes the event.
  - This is page 2's "one way update flow keeps everyone on same page", in code.
- **Real time.** A FastAPI WebSocket at `/api/modules/fieldops/ws`.
  - Each client subscribes to topics: its role's queue, its own tech queue, or a single job.
  - The hub runs in process. Tailor's backend runs one uvicorn process (`templates/backend/Dockerfile`, no `--workers`),
    so no broker is needed.
  - Caddy's `reverse_proxy` passes the WebSocket upgrade through, edge Caddy included. We verify that in the dry run
    (§9, risk R1).
- **No missed updates.** Every event carries the `job_events.id`. On reconnect, a view sends its last id and the
  backend replays what it missed from `job_events`. So the views stay honest after a dropped connection (house
  convention: honest systems).
- **The record is the state.** The overview and the queues are queries over `jobs`. `job_events` is the history, and
  gives turnaround per step (V.1, Cost of Delay).
- **Rails the backend enforces:**
  - A job over its contract cap cannot be dispatched until the approval is on record.
  - A job cannot close without a sign-off or a reason.
  - An invoice cannot be approved for a job that failed the check.
  - A person can override anything else. The override is recorded.

## 5. The data model (structured data, from `2026-11-12-job-record-draft.md`)

Own-table module `fieldops`: tables prefixed `mod_fieldops_*` with their own Alembic branch (Tailor ADR-0038, ADR-0040).

| Table | Key fields | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `clients` | name, channel (portal, email, facilit), invoice_format | ~6 synthetic clients |
| `contracts` | client, issue-type caps, approval rules (JSON: cap by issue type, review triggers), net terms | **Read-only to the job** (page 2: "Checked before work assigned") |
| `sites` | client, store number, address, state (PA, OH, NY, NJ, DE, WV, MD), lat/lng, site contact | |
| `people` | name, ops role (coordinator, dispatcher, tech, controller), Tailor user link | Demo accounts |
| `techs` | person, skills, trade licenses by state, home base, truck | ~10 |
| `fleet_positions` | truck, lat/lng, at, status | Simulated Samsara |
| `jobs` | **A identity:** client, site, client WO and PO, site contact, source. **B working:** issue type, parts (none, on truck, special order), part numbers, estimated hours (from like jobs), actual hours, extenuating (list plus a line). **C approval:** requires approval, rule fired, approval (who, when, client reference). **D state:** state, owner coordinator, assigned tech, scheduled window, sign-off, estimate amount, final amount | Replaces the notes field |
| `job_events` | job, at, actor (person or simulator), command, from_state, to_state, payload | Append-only; feeds the websocket and the replay |
| `invoices` | job, lines, total, format, state (draft, approved, submitted), qb_doc_number | Drafted at close |
| `qb_ledger` | invoice, doc number, amount, issued, due, paid | **The simulated QuickBooks.** Aging computed from it |

**States** (page 2, left to right):

`intake_draft` → `qualified` → (`needs_client_approval` ↔ `qualified`) → `ready_for_dispatch` → `assigned` → `onsite`
→ (`needs_review` → `assigned` / `ready_for_dispatch` / `onsite`) → `closed` → `billing_review` → `invoiced` (submitted
to QuickBooks) → `paid`

## 6. The views (Tailor React Router 7 app, under `/ops`)

| Route | View (page 4) | What it shows | Actions |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/ops` | Overview | Every job by queue and state: counts per state, then a table with filters | Open a job |
| `/ops/coordinator` | 1 Coordinator workspace | Queues: drafts, mine, needs approval, needs review. Opens one job record with typed fields, the contract panel (cap, rules, terms, read-only) and history | Edit fields, request client approval, record approval, approve for dispatch, decide on needs review |
| `/ops/dispatch` | 2 Dispatch | Ready for dispatch, with the recommended tech and reasons (skills, license in state, distance from the truck, load); techs on a simple map or list; needs review | Assign (approve the recommendation or pick), reassign, ask to wait |
| `/ops/tech` | 2 Field app (phone layout) | My schedule: today's jobs in order, the current job's typed fields and contract rails | Arrive, update fields, needs review (reason), sign-off and close |
| `/ops/controller` | 3 Controller queue | Billing queue (closed jobs with drafted invoices, check results), approved and submitted invoices, the QuickBooks aging summary | Approve invoice (auto-submits), send back with a reason |

- **Live everywhere.** A changed row flashes once and settles, so the eye catches the update. That's the demo's point.
- **The house UI canon applies (PQ-9..34).** Skeletons rather than spinners, no layout shift and no fake loading
  states. The tech view is designed phone first.
- **Demo identity.** Seeded accounts per role, with a role switcher on a `/ops/demo` page so one person can drive
  every view. A real login stays in front (Orly).

## 7. The simulation (realistic enough to make each point, not every detail)

Jen's generator, deterministic from a seed, built the way the podcast pipeline's `fixtures_gen` builds:
- **Clients and contracts:** ~6 synthetic clients.
  - One portal-only national chain (strict format, $25K cap without prior approval, from round 4).
  - One coffee chain sending store emails.
  - Several smaller FacilIT clients.
  - Names are fictional by default (gap Q4).
- **Sites:** ~60 sites across the seven states.
- **People:** ~10 techs with skills and state licenses, 3 coordinators, 1 dispatcher (Tony is the client's real staff
  in the role play, so the demo uses role names or new names, gap Q5), and 1 controller.
- **Jobs at the start:** ~45 spread over every state, so the overview is full on first load. Each has a history in
  `job_events`, so turnaround shows. Values run $2K–$50K (Dana). A few sit over the cap.
- **Arrivals:**
  - The simulator drafts a new intake job from a synthetic email or portal record about every 45 seconds while the
    demo runs.
  - Drafting is rule-based: fields extracted from a template. It carries a "Drafted from source" label. That's page 3's
    gear, shown honestly.
  - An LLM drafter sits behind a flag only if John says Claude is allowed (gap Q3).
- **Fleet:** truck positions move along simple paths every 10 s. The recommendation reads them.
- **Recommendation:** a transparent score. Hard filters: the skill and the license in the site's state. Then rank by
  distance from the truck plus current load. The UI shows the top choice and why. Jen's eval: 20 hand-checked cases.
- **QuickBooks:**
  - Approving an invoice writes to `qb_ledger` with a doc number, as if posted.
  - Aging is computed from ~150 seeded historical invoices with realistic due dates and payments.
  - Labelled "QuickBooks (simulated)".
- **Controls:** `/ops/demo` has pause, resume and reset (re-seed), so every demo starts the same.

## 11. Demo script (5 minutes, four windows: coordinator, dispatch, tech phone, controller, with the overview in a tab)

1. **Overview:** every job by state. "One record, every queue." *(G3)*
2. **A draft arrives**, drafted from source. The coordinator checks the typed fields and sees the contract cap: this
   one is over it. Record the client approval, then approve for dispatch. *(C2, C3)*
3. **Dispatch sees it arrive.** It shows the recommended tech and why. Approve. *(D1 to D3)*
4. **The tech's phone shows it** on the schedule. The tech arrives, finds a scope change and sets **needs review**.
   Dispatch and the coordinator both see it at once. Dispatch reassigns the tech to a ready job and asks this one to
   wait. *(T1, T3, T4)*
5. **The tech closes a job with sign-off.** It lands in the controller's queue with a drafted invoice in the client's
   format. Approve: it is submitted to QuickBooks and the aging updates. *(T2, B1 to B3)*
6. **Close:** "Every change came from one person in one view, and everyone else saw it the instant it happened. That's
   the dispute prevented, not chased." *(G1, the message)*
