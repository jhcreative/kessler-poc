### Step 1: Intake (John, 2026-11-12)

- Clients are on **2–5 year contracts** won in a separate process, so intake starts from known customers. Several have
  very specific ways of doing business.
- **Target:** OneFamilyServices staff log into Target's portal (email and password) to pick up work.
- **Starbucks:** sends email from an address that identifies the store.
- **Others:** a mix of email and attachments to start a request.
- OF staff **enter all of it by hand** into **FieldDesk**, the SaaS source of truth.
- **FacilIT:** another SaaS, used by only some clients, which OF pays to connect to. To research.
- Intake is **complete when the request lands with a coordinator**, who owns it for its whole life.

### Step 2: Coordination (John, 2026-11-12)

- **Three people** handle intake at central HQ; **20,000 requests a quarter** (about 300 a working day, Angela's arithmetic).
- Every request is assigned to a coordinator, who owns it.
- Requests are accurate enough when received. **Detail is lost in the handling**, because people are overwhelmed.
- The pattern repeats across steps, **for lack of structured data**: cognitive overload means complex job state goes
  into empty notes fields, and key information is lost: the diagnosis, what the contract covers, price caps by issue type.
- Loss runs both ways: coordinators don't always get what they need from techs (onsite reports, estimates), and techs
  don't always get what they need for estimating (client specifics, price caps).

### Step 3: Estimation (John, 2026-11-12)

- Missing information leads to **work done without approval**.
- Pictures and summaries are **texted from personal phones**: no central place to build case history or verify decisions.
- **No gates** from known customer limits or each customer's own approval pattern.
- No place to keep what was learned or proven fixes, except people's heads (tribal knowledge).

### Step 4: Dispatch (John, 2026-11-12)

- Run by **one person, Tony**, a single point of failure, on his personal phone, with **Samsara** (SaaS fleet management).
  To research Samsara and what it can do.
- Every truck carries a **parts inventory, managed on a spreadsheet somewhere**; nobody really knows how standard and
  common parts (chips and clips) flow.

### Step 5: Onsite (John, 2026-11-12)

- Similar: personal phones, nowhere to capture structured data, store images, or build case and client history that
  could improve future projections or **reclaim revenue from disputed invoices**.
- No inventory beyond new parts ordered for a specific request, and those have to be tracked to the job by hand, more
  data entry that overwhelmed people often skip.
- **The generic notes field fails hardest here.**

### The leak, in dollars (John, 2026-11-12)

- **$80,000 to $200,000 a year in disputed invoices** for work not approved before it was done.
- Scale (Angela's arithmetic): 20,000 requests a quarter is about 80,000 a year, so $1 to $2.50 per request on average.
- Job size: **$2,000 to $50,000** (Dana).
- The figure is **Dana's**. OF **takes the loss in accounting**: written off.
- John's read: it's **contractual knowledge that has to reach the tech** so they don't go off the reservation. If Target
  caps requests at **$25,000 without prior authorization** (John's example), the coordinator and the tech should know
  going in, and so should the system. The system should also **rate a tech's estimate against the history of like
  jobs**, given how much data the company could be collecting in a well-defined space.

### Channels by client size (John, 2026-11-12)

- **Target** is too big for any shared platform: "do it their way or get lost." Work comes only through Target's portal.
- **Smaller companies** use the FacilIT connection.

### FieldDesk and FacilIT, John's read (2026-11-12)

- **FacilIT** is probably a necessary dependency.
- **FieldDesk** stores the truth but doesn't expose it: nobody can know the **state of a request** at any given time,
  there's **no data on turnaround**, nor the other detail that goes with excellent client and opportunity management.

### Step 6: Close (John, 2026-11-12)

- Work complete is registered as such in **FieldDesk**, which somehow triggers billing.

### Step 7: Billing (John, 2026-11-12)

- **One person, Marcee**, a single point of failure, does it all by hand. She pulls completed work out of FieldDesk
  (how: to research) and matches it against contract specifics, POs, the signature of completion from the field, and so on.

### Step 8: Invoicing (John, 2026-11-12)

- Marcee enters invoices into **QuickBooks**, then delivers each in the client's preferred format (Target: their way
  or no work).

### Step 9: Collections and aging (John, 2026-11-12)

- Aging reports from QuickBooks and **a few spreadsheets** are the whole system.
- **Marcee is the only enforcer**; not much happens when she's unavailable.
