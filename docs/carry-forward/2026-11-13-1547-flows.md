## How we got here: the thought process, in order

**1. Intake, 2026-11-12.** We went through the nine steps one at a time, Socratically, as John asked.
- The facts are in `docs/meetings/2026-11-12-round-4-discovery.md` and the answers in
  `docs/plans/2026-11-12-intake-questions.md`.
- The leak: $80K–$200K a year in disputes, written off. The cause: approval isn't on record before the work.

**2. Architecture, 2026-11-12. Decision FR.1:** a harness with adapters, not a vendor core.
- John's reason: Target has its own way in and out, and he wants no dependence on a paid subscription.
- **FR.2:** the FacilIT questions FI.1–FI.7 wait for a later round.
- **FR.3:** one field app for every tech and every job.

**3. First portrait deck, 2026-11-12.** It opened on the architecture. John: "way too simple… first impression needs
to be flow of single request through a system where we can see the brittle/missing parts and measure Cost of Delay…
multiple efficiencies adding up in your process over time - not 'new master system that will fix everything'". That
became the engagement's message (CLAUDE.md) and a team memory.
- Data written: `docs/flow/one-request.yaml` (9 breaks, Cost of Delay, 7 efficiencies),
  `request-to-onsite.yaml` (draft 3, contracts as a separate store) and `close-to-cash.yaml`.
- Those portrait pages are now drafts, in `docs/flow/drafts/`.

**4. Landscape, left to right, 2026-11-13.** John art-directed one change at a time. The shape of it:
- **A flow, not an architecture.** Three ways in feed Job records. The record reads a separate Contracts store, and the
  arrow points into the record.
- **The loop.** The record goes out to Dispatch and Onsite, which update it back. Only after that does it move on to
  Billing, Invoicing and Collections.
- **Additional approvals** go out of the record and back in, before dispatch.
- **The current page** was redrawn as today:
  - coordinators re-key every request;
  - the tech texts Tony, and Tony updates the record;
  - Marcee reconciles and invoices by hand;
  - disputes loop back through contracts to the coordinators.
- **Motifs** arrived in this order:
  1. manual "by hand" junctions;
  2. the human-review chip, with a person icon and a role;
  3. stacked sheets for many formats;
  4. the black automation gear, with captions of two lines or fewer.
- **The intro lines** became sisters: the human burden today, then what the recommended flow does about it.
- The full direction is captured as team canon in
  `~/jhc/jhc-agency/skills/product-ops/report-writer/references/flow-diagrams.md`.

- **The flows took a build day.** They are the understanding Ann-Marie will judge first, and the POC plan now has a
  picture to build against.

## Lessons

- **Open on the story, not the system.** One request, its breaks, and what each wait costs.
- **Data first, then the drawing.** Every word on the drawings traces back to discovery or to a marked proposal.
- **Render and look every time.** Most fixes were overlaps that only the rasterized page showed.
