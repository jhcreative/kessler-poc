What Each Wait Costs

Discovery Report•OneFamilyServices•November 13, 2026
Where Today Cost of delay What we know We'd measure
1 Intake Requests re-keyed by hand from Target's portal, store emails and FacilIT into FieldDesk. Every hour a request waits to reach a coordinator comes off the client's clock. 3 people; 20,000 requests a quarter, about 300 a working day. Time from the client's request to a coordinator owning it.
2 Job record Detail goes into a notes field and is lost in the handling, the diagnosis, the coverage, the caps. Each later step re-asks or guesses; rework, and decisions made without the facts. Requests arrive accurate; the loss is in the handling (Dana). Jobs where a later step asks for something already received.
3 Qualified No gate. Caps and approval rules live in people's heads. Work done without approval becomes a dispute, written off. $80K to $200K a year (Dana): $1,500 to $3,800 a week. Jobs over a cap with no approval on record.
4 Estimation Photos and summaries texted from personal phones; no history of like jobs. An estimate can't be checked before the work starts. Jobs run $2,000 to $50,000 (Dana). Estimate against final, by job type.
5 Dispatch One dispatcher on his personal phone, with Samsara; truck parts on a spreadsheet. When he's out, dispatch waits; a missing part means a return visit. A single point of failure. Return visits for parts; days dispatch runs without him.
6 Onsite The need for approval is found onsite and chased after the work. The dispute in break 3, born here. See break 3. Time from "needs approval" to approved.
7 Close to billing Completed work pulled from FieldDesk by hand; timing varies with volume. Every day from close to invoice is a day the cash arrives late. To measure. Days from close to invoice, times the job's value.
8 Invoice One person; about a dozen strict client formats; re-keyed into QuickBooks. When she's out, invoices wait; a wrong format is a refused invoice. A single point of failure. Invoices refused, and why.
9 Paid Aging in QuickBooks and a few spreadsheets; disputes surface after it's all done. Written off. See break 3. Disputes by cause.

Nobody can measure any of this today

FieldDesk holds the truth but shows nobody the state of a request; there is no turnaround data. So the first efficiency is the clock: a state and a timestamp for every request at every step. Every number above that reads "to measure" starts there.

Source: docs/flow/one-request.yaml. Figures are Dana's; the weekly range is her yearly figure over 52 weeks.
