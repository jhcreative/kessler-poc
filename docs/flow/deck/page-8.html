Flow 2, Today and Proposed

Discovery Report•OneFamilyServices•November 13, 2026
Step Today Proposed
Closestep 6 Work complete is registered in FieldDesk, which somehow triggers billing. The client's local rep signs off in the field. The field app closes a job only with the signature, or with the reason it's missing. Closing puts the job into billing; nobody has to go and get it.
Billingstep 7 One person pulls completed work from FieldDesk, timing varying with volume, and matches it by hand to the contract, the PO and the signature. The system makes the match: approval on record when a rule required it, signature, PO, amount within the cap. She sees only what fails.
Invoicingstep 8 The same person keys each invoice into QuickBooks, then sends it in the client's format. About a dozen clients set strict ones. One invoice from the job record, written out by each client's adapter, with the approval and signature attached. QuickBooks gets it without re-keying.
Collectionsstep 9 QuickBooks aging and a few spreadsheets. The same person is the only one who enforces; when she's out, little happens. Every dispute is recorded against its job, with the rule that should have fired, and fed back to flow 1.

Why this flow matters

Aging isn't the concern; disputes are. Once the work is done, the company invoices even when the signature is missing or the contract is vague, because it's the only way to get paid. The check before the invoice makes that gap visible while it can still be closed.

Still open

  • How a close in FieldDesk triggers billing today, and whether its data can get out.
  • Each strict-format client's invoice rules: Target, CVS, Walgreens, Wegmans, ALDI.
  • How the disputes split: no approval on record, a missing signature, a vague contract.
