Lifecycle of a Service Request

Proof of Concept•Kessler Facility Services•November 16, 2026

Working from discovery notes, we started by mapping the lifecycle of a service request through the 9 stages of the current Kessler Facility Services (KFS) systems, from original intake to payment collection.

Guided by the observation that different parts of the system "don't really talk to each other", our starting goal was to find these hand-off points and look into how we can improve performance of each one to reduce waste and speed the work.

In addition, we evaluated each part and the whole together for future sustainability and ability to support planned KFS expansion and subsequent increased volume of requests and services provided.

1SYSTEM MAP

Nine steps, one request at a time.

Page 2 documents intake, coordination, estimation, dispatch, onsite, close, billing, invoicing and collections to the best of our current understanding. Further deep dives required before moving beyond this POC.

2OPPORTUNITIES

People carry the current system by hand.

Page 6 captures 9 system hand-offs that are currently manual events, where given request data is re-keyed multiple times across its own lifecycle. These hand-off points are our main opportunities for system performance improvement and both time & money savings in immediate, short and long terms.

3SOLUTION

Single source of truth for everyone.

Page 3 introduces our thesis that unifying all system inputs into a single source of truth, visible & interactive in real-time to Coordinator, Dispatch, Tech, Controller and Executive alike, is the best solution for the future KFS has planned.

Pages 7-9 detail the POC application design we built to demonstrate that thesis, using synthetic data to demonstrate potential KFS request lifecycle across roles, assuming no dependency or integration with current SaaS investments.
